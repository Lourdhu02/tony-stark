    References · Mark III · The Kernel#

    Operating systems, concurrency, memory models and I/O.

    18 works · difficulty: ★☆☆ accessible · ★★☆ technical · ★★★ research-grade
    Read with the three-pass method. Every link is a Google Scholar search for the exact title. Master index →

    Start here#

    ★☆☆ Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces#

    Arpaci-Dusseau & Arpaci-Dusseau · 2018 · Arpaci-Dusseau Books (free online) · book · Scholar ↗

    • Why: Clear, short chapters on virtualization and concurrency.
    • Read: Parts I–II.

    ★★☆ xv6: a simple, Unix-like teaching operating system#

    Cox, Kaashoek & Morris · 2019 · MIT PDOS · book · Scholar ↗

    • Why: A real kernel, small enough to read completely.
    • Read: Ch. 1–5.

    ★★☆ Rust Atomics and Locks#

    Mara Bos · 2023 · O'Reilly (free online) · book · Scholar ↗

    • Why: Memory ordering and lock-free primitives, done properly.
    • Read: Ch. 1–6.

    Landmarks#

    ★★☆ Cooperating sequential processes#

    Edsger Dijkstra · 1965 · Technical report EWD123 · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: Semaphores, mutual exclusion and the dining philosophers: concurrency's founding document.
    • Read: The first half.

    ★★☆ The structure of the "THE"-multiprogramming system#

    Edsger Dijkstra · 1968 · Communications of the ACM · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: The layered OS design that introduced structured systems thinking.
    • Read: All of it.

    ★★☆ Time, clocks, and the ordering of events in a distributed system#

    Leslie Lamport · 1978 · Communications of the ACM · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: Happens-before: the causal ordering underneath every memory model.
    • Read: All of it.

    ★★☆ How to make a multiprocessor computer that correctly executes multiprocess programs#

    Leslie Lamport · 1979 · IEEE Transactions on Computers · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: The definition of sequential consistency, in 2 pages.
    • Read: All of it.

    ★★★ Scheduling algorithms for multiprogramming in a hard-real-time environment#

    Liu & Layland · 1973 · Journal of the ACM · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: Rate-monotonic scheduling and the utilization bound. The basis of real-time systems.
    • Read: The first theorems.

    ★★★ Priority inheritance protocols: An approach to real-time synchronization#

    Sha, Rajkumar & Lehoczky · 1990 · IEEE Transactions on Computers · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: The priority inheritance protocol: the fix for the Mars Pathfinder bug.
    • Read: §1–3.

    Lock-free and scheduling#

    ★★★ Wait-free synchronization#

    Maurice Herlihy · 1991 · ACM TOPLAS · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: Wait-free synchronization and consensus numbers: why CAS is universal.
    • Read: §1–4.

    ★★☆ Simple, fast, and practical non-blocking and blocking concurrent queue algorithms#

    Michael & Scott · 1996 · PODC · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: The Michael–Scott queue, the canonical lock-free queue.
    • Read: All of it.

    ★★★ Foundations of the C++ concurrency memory model#

    Boehm & Adve · 2008 · PLDI · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: How the C++ (and Rust) memory model was designed.
    • Read: §1–4.

    ★★★ Scheduling multithreaded computations by work stealing#

    Blumofe & Leiserson · 1999 · Journal of the ACM · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: Work stealing, with provable bounds.
    • Read: §1–3.

    ★★☆ Dynamic circular work-stealing deque#

    Chase & Lev · 2005 · SPAA · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: The work-stealing deque used by Rayon, Tokio and friends.
    • Read: All of it.

    Frontier#

    ★★★ seL4: formal verification of an OS kernel#

    Klein et al. · 2009 · SOSP · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: A kernel with a machine-checked proof of correctness.
    • Read: §1–3.

    ★★☆ Exokernel: an operating system architecture for application-level resource management#

    Engler, Kaashoek & O'Toole · 1995 · SOSP · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: Exokernels: securely exposing hardware to applications.
    • Read: §1–3.

    ★☆☆ The C10K problem#

    Dan Kegel · 1999 · Web article · essay · Scholar ↗

    • Why: Why epoll exists.
    • Read: All of it.

    ★★☆ Efficient IO with io_uring#

    Jens Axboe · 2019 · Kernel documentation (kernel.dk) · essay · Scholar ↗

    • Why: io_uring: asynchronous I/O through shared ring buffers (your SPSC ring, in the kernel).
    • Read: All of it.
