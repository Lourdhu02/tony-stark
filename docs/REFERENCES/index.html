    References · The knowledge base#

    The science of learning hard things and the philosophy of the field. These shape how you study everything else.

    11 works · difficulty: ★☆☆ accessible · ★★☆ technical · ★★★ research-grade
    Read with the three-pass method. Every link is a Google Scholar search for the exact title. Master index →

    Start here#

    ★☆☆ How to read a paper#

    Keshav · 2007 · ACM SIGCOMM Computer Communication Review · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: The three-pass method for reading any paper. Every REFERENCES.md in this repo assumes it.
    • Read: All of it (2 pages). Use pass 1 on every paper before deciding to go deeper.

    ★☆☆ You and Your Research#

    Richard Hamming · 1986 · Talk at Bell Communications Research (transcript) · talk · Scholar ↗

    • Why: Why some engineers do great work and most don't: important problems, open doors, compounding knowledge. The spirit of this lab.
    • Read: All of it. Re-read it once a year.

    ★★☆ The role of deliberate practice in the acquisition of expert performance#

    Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer · 1993 · Psychological Review · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: The origin of deliberate practice: expertise comes from effortful, feedback-rich practice at the edge of ability, not from hours logged.
    • Read: The introduction and the section on the characteristics of deliberate practice.

    How memory works (why the method works)#

    ★☆☆ Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention#

    Roediger & Karpicke · 2006 · Psychological Science · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: The testing effect: retrieval practice beats re-reading for long-term retention. It's why every dossier has a quiz.
    • Read: The experiments and the discussion.

    ★★☆ Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis#

    Cepeda et al. · 2006 · Psychological Bulletin · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: A meta-analysis of spacing: distributed review beats massed review. It's why the method has a Sunday spaced-review slot.
    • Read: The abstract and the discussion; skim the tables.

    ★★☆ Memory and metamemory considerations in the training of human beings#

    Bjork · 1994 · In Metacognition: Knowing about Knowing (MIT Press) · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: Desirable difficulties: conditions that slow learning in the moment (deriving before reading, interleaving) improve retention.
    • Read: The sections on desirable difficulties.

    ★★☆ Self-explanations: How students study and use examples in learning to solve problems#

    Chi et al. · 1989 · Cognitive Science · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: Self-explanation: students who explain worked examples to themselves learn far more. It's why the lab note has a Learned section.
    • Read: The results section.

    ★☆☆ Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques: Promising directions from cognitive and educational psychology#

    Dunlosky et al. · 2013 · Psychological Science in the Public Interest · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: A rating of ten study techniques by evidence. Practice testing and distributed practice win; highlighting and re-reading lose.
    • Read: The summary table, then the two top-rated techniques.

    The shape of the field#

    ★☆☆ Mind Children: The Future of Robot and Human Intelligence#

    Hans Moravec · 1988 · Harvard University Press · book · Scholar ↗

    • Why: Moravec's paradox: what is hard for humans (chess) is easy for machines, and what is easy for humans (walking, grasping) is hard. It's why robotics is its own discipline.
    • Read: The chapters on robot perception and mobility.

    ★★☆ Intelligence without representation#

    Rodney Brooks · 1991 · Artificial Intelligence · paper · Scholar ↗

    • Why: The behaviour-based challenge to classical AI: intelligence emerges from situated interaction with the world.
    • Read: All of it: short and provocative.

    ★☆☆ The Bitter Lesson#

    Rich Sutton · 2019 · Essay (incompleteideas.net) · essay · Scholar ↗

    • Why: General methods that leverage computation win in the long run. Read it against Brooks, then decide where robotics sits today.
    • Read: All of it (1 page).
